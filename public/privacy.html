Privacy, in plain words

BinaryBond is designed so that we can't learn much about you even if we wanted to. This page explains exactly what the server handles, what it keeps, and what it never sees.

Short version: no accounts for callers, no cookies, no analytics, no third-party scripts, no recordings. Calls, messages and files go directly between participants' devices, end-to-end encrypted.

What never reaches the server

  • Your audio, video and screen shares.
  • Chat messages, reactions and files.
  • Your display name (it is encrypted with the room key).
  • The room key or the invite link. Browsers don't send the part after # to servers.
  • Connection details such as SDP offers and network candidates (sealed with AES-256-GCM).

What the server handles while you're connected

  • A room ID derived one-way from your room key, and a hash of a membership token.
  • Your IP address, for the duration of the WebSocket connection, because that's how the internet works. It's used in memory for rate limiting and isn't written to disk or logs.
  • Encrypted signaling messages, which are relayed and immediately discarded.

What the server keeps

  • Daily totals: how many rooms were created, how many participant-minutes were used, and peak room size. There is no link to rooms, people or IPs.
  • For developer workspaces only: workspace name, contact email, a SHA-256 hash of the API key, plan, branding, webhook URL and allowed domains.

Peer-to-peer and your IP address

When devices connect directly, the other participants in your call can see your IP address, the same as in any peer-to-peer app. If that's a concern, turn on Hide my IP (when the server offers a relay). Your media then goes through a TURN relay that only sees encrypted packets.

Third parties

The app loads nothing from other domains: no fonts, no CDNs, no analytics. To find a direct route between devices, browsers may contact a STUN server. Operators can run their own (BinaryBond includes one) so that no outside party is involved. If a workspace uses paid plans, billing is processed by Stripe on Stripe's own pages.

Local storage on your device

Your preferences (theme, name, devices, filters) are stored in your browser. The current room key is kept in session storage, which disappears when you close the tab. Panic wipe (Shift+Esc) erases all of it instantly.

Your rights

Because callers have no accounts and no personal data is stored, there's normally nothing to access or delete. Developer workspaces can be deleted on request by the server operator, which removes the workspace record permanently.
